## Two kinds of chats

Your team may have two kinds of threads:

- **Shared chat.** It belongs to the team. Colleagues can read it, see the files it produced and continue it. If you go on holiday, someone else picks up where you left off.
- **Personal chat.** It is yours. Nobody else can read it or write in it. Not a colleague, not your manager.

Neither is better. They are for different things.

:::picture shared
A shared chat for the team, and a personal one just for you.
:::

## What goes where

Use a **shared chat** for team work: the monthly newsletter, the press release for the launch, the client offer the whole sales team follows. Anything a colleague might need to continue or check. Work that should stay with the company when people change roles.

Use a **personal chat** for what is only yours: drafts you are not ready to show, trying things out, learning, questions you would rather not ask in front of others. And anything about you personally: your appraisal, a message to HR, a leave request.

:::tip
A simple test: if you were away tomorrow, should a colleague be able to open this chat and continue? If yes, shared. If it would be awkward for them to see it, personal.
:::

## Personal things stay out of shared chats

A shared chat is like a meeting room with glass walls. Your colleagues see everything you write there, including the files you attach and what the agent answers.

:::warn
No personal details, no opinions about colleagues, no salary questions, no private documents in a shared chat.
:::

If you notice you started something personal in a shared chat, stop and move to a personal one.

## A colleague's personal chat

You might want to send a message into a colleague's personal chat: "Just add the November numbers to Maria's report, she's out today." The app does not allow it, for good reasons:

- **It runs with her access.** The agent in Maria's chat can reach the apps and folders Maria connected. Your message would act with her permissions, not yours.
- **It uses her memory.** The agent remembers Maria's context and preferences. Your request would mix into it, and she would find changes she did not ask for.
- **It is her responsibility.** Whatever happens there is assumed to be Maria's work. She should not answer for something you sent.
- **It is her workspace.** Like a personal notebook on someone's desk: you don't write in it, even if it is open.

## What to do instead

:::dont
You try to write in Maria's personal chat while she is away.
:::

:::do
You ask Maria to make the chat shared (or move the work to a shared one). Or you start a new shared chat yourself, add the files you need and send her the link.
:::

If it is urgent and she is away, ask your manager to decide where the work continues. A manager cannot read personal chats either, and that is on purpose.

## Try it yourself

No button for this one. Look at your last five threads and sort them: which should be shared, and which stay personal?

For one that should be shared, start a shared chat, add the files and a short summary of where the work stands, then tell your colleagues where to find it.

You are writing a leave request with the agent's help. Where do you do it?

- [ ] In a shared chat, so your manager sees it too.
- [x] In a personal chat.

> Anything about you personally stays in a personal chat.
:::
