# New-employee onboarding kit

A good first week answers, in order: where do I go and who meets me, what do I do today, who
do I ask, how do things work here, and how will I know I am doing well.

## welcome-guide.md (for the newcomer; warm, plain, 2-3 pages)

1. A short welcome from the manager (5-6 sentences, no corporate phrases).
2. **Day one**: address and entrance, time, who meets them, what to bring (ID, bank account
   details for salary, medical certificate if not yet done, and anything the form lists),
   dress code, where to park or which bus, lunch.
3. **Who is who**: the people they will work with, by role, with how to reach them.
4. **How we work**: schedule and breaks, how shifts or tasks are assigned, the tools and
   accounts they get, how to ask for leave, what to do when sick, the 5-8 house rules that
   matter (from the documents; never invented).
5. **Your first month**: what success looks like at 30 days, in two or three sentences.
6. Glossary of the words used in the company (only if the documents use internal terms).

## first-week-plan.md (for the newcomer and the manager)

A table per day (Day 1 to Day 5): time | what | with whom | goal. Day 1 is mostly welcome,
paperwork, safety, tour and shadowing; by Day 3 the newcomer does a small real task with help;
Day 5 ends with a 20-minute talk with the manager (what went well, what is unclear, next
week). Realistic for a small team: one buddy, short sessions, no 8-hour training days.

## checklists.md

- **Before day one (manager / admin)**. For Romania, as applicable to the job (mark each
  "[CHECK] confirm with your accountant or HR provider" when the company uses one):
  individual employment contract signed and registered in REVISAL at the latest the day
  before work starts; medical check at the occupational doctor (fișa de aptitudine) before
  starting; health and safety (SSM) and fire (SU/PSI) training on the first day, recorded in
  the individual training sheet; job description (fișa postului) signed; GDPR information for
  employees; for food businesses, the food hygiene course certificate if required for the
  role. Then the practical part: accounts, keys, uniform, locker, equipment, place to sit,
  team told about the arrival.
- **Day one (newcomer and buddy)**: a tick list.
- **End of week one**: what the newcomer should be able to do alone.
- **30 / 60 / 90 days**: 2-3 measurable goals each, from the role.

## manager-notes.md

What to prepare, what to say on day one, the buddy's job, how to give feedback in week one,
the questions for the day-5 talk, the signs that onboarding is not working.

## Rules

- Use only the company's documents, its memory and the form. A rule, a tool or a person that
  is not there is not written; a missing practical detail is [CHECK: ...].
- Legal items above are general reminders, not legal advice; never state an amount, a deadline
  or a penalty that is not in this guidance or the company's documents.
- Write to the newcomer with "tu" unless the company's documents use "dumneavoastră".
  Romanian with ș and ț (comma below). No emoji.

## Self-review (do it, then fix what fails)

1. Every name, rule, tool and address comes from the documents, the memory or the form.
2. Day one is complete: where, when, who, what to bring.
3. The week plan has a real task by day 3 and a manager talk on day 5.
4. The Romanian legal items are in the before-day-one checklist and marked to confirm.
5. 30/60/90 goals are specific to the role and measurable.
6. Missing details are [CHECK] and listed at the end of your reply.
